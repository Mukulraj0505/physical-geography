Earth's Magnetic Field and the Geodynamo

A magnetic field is the region around a magnet or moving electric charge in which magnetic forces can act. Earth behaves approximately like a giant magnetic dipole, producing a magnetic field that extends far into space.

Earth's magnetic field is generated mainly by the geodynamo operating in the liquid outer core.

Important correction: The magnetic field is not generated by convection in the solid inner core. The outer core is liquid and rich in molten iron and nickel; its convection, together with Earth's rotation and the Coriolis force, sustains the geodynamo.
Earth's internal heat → convection of electrically conducting liquid iron in outer core → motion of charged/conducting fluid → electric currents → magnetic field → interaction of magnetic field with fluid motion → self-sustaining geodynamo

The geodynamo is therefore a combined consequence of:

  • Convection in the liquid outer core
  • Electrical conductivity of molten iron
  • Earth's rotation
  • Coriolis force, which organises fluid motion
  • Continued removal of heat from the core and other processes that help sustain convection

Why Earth's magnetic field matters

Solar wind + energetic charged particles → encounter Earth's magnetic field → much of the charged-particle flow is deflected around or channelled toward polar regions → protection of atmosphere and technological systems
  • Helps shield Earth from a portion of the solar wind and energetic charged particles.
  • Helps protect the upper atmosphere from atmospheric erosion caused by solar particles.
  • Enables compass navigation.
  • Produces conditions for auroras.
  • Provides information about Earth's interior through magnetic measurements.
  • Influences space weather and its effects on satellites, communications, navigation and power systems.

Magnetic Poles, Magnetic Equator and Field Elements

Earth's magnetic field is not perfectly aligned with its geographic axis. Therefore, magnetic poles and geographic poles do not coincide.

Magnetic Poles

In practical geomagnetic terminology, a magnetic/dip pole is a region where the magnetic field is approximately vertical and the magnetic inclination is close to 90°.

The magnetic poles move continuously because Earth's magnetic field changes over time.

Modern observations show that the magnetic field varies both spatially and temporally. The northern magnetic dip pole currently moves much faster than the southern one. NOAA's 2025 assessment reported average movement since January 2025 of about 36 km/year for the North magnetic dip pole and about 9 km/year for the South magnetic dip pole. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

Magnetic Equator

The magnetic equator is the line where the magnetic inclination is approximately 0°.

It is not the same as the geographic Equator and its position changes gradually with changes in Earth's magnetic field.

Magnetic poles → maximum vertical component → magnetic inclination ≈ 90°
Magnetic equator → horizontal magnetic field → magnetic inclination ≈ 0°
Important fact: At the magnetic poles, the compass becomes unreliable for horizontal direction because the horizontal component of the magnetic field approaches zero. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

Magnetic field elements

Element Meaning
Magnetic declination (D) Angle between true/geographic north and magnetic north.
Magnetic inclination / dip (I) Angle made by Earth's magnetic field with the horizontal plane.
Magnetic intensity Strength of the Earth's magnetic field at a location.

Declination:

Magnetic north east of true north → positive/easterly declination
Magnetic north west of true north → negative/westerly declination

Inclination:

Magnetic equator → dip ≈ 0° → field nearly horizontal
Magnetic poles → dip ≈ 90° → field nearly vertical

NOAA defines positive declination as magnetic north lying east of true north and magnetic inclination as the angle between the field vector and the horizontal, measured positive downward into Earth. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

Magnetic Poles, Geomagnetic Poles and Geographic Poles

Aspect Magnetic / Dip Poles Geomagnetic Poles Geographic Poles
Basis Observed magnetic field / field inclination Idealised dipole model of Earth's magnetic field Earth's rotational axis
Nature Physical locations where the field is approximately vertical Mathematical/modelled dipole locations North and South ends of Earth's rotation axis
Stability Move over time Also vary as the field changes Much more stable

The geomagnetic dipole axis is currently tilted by roughly 11° from Earth's rotational axis.

2005 UPSC Prelims: UPSC tested the distinction between the geographic and magnetic systems and used the approximate 11° tilt of Earth's magnetic dipole axis as a key fact. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

Compass

A compass needle aligns itself with the local horizontal component of Earth's magnetic field.

Compass needle → aligns with local magnetic field → indicates magnetic north, not exact geographic north

Therefore, navigation requires correction for magnetic declination.

Magnetic declination vs magnetic deviation

Term Meaning
Declination Difference between true north and magnetic north at a place.
Deviation Error/deflection of a compass caused by nearby magnetic materials, electrical equipment or the vehicle/vessel itself.

Geomagnetic Reversal

A geomagnetic reversal is a large-scale change in Earth's magnetic polarity in which the magnetic field's dipole orientation reverses.

Normal polarity → magnetic field orientation → geomagnetic reversal → reversed polarity

During reversed polarity, the magnetic polarity is opposite to the present configuration.

The present polarity has persisted for approximately 780,000 years, but reversals do not occur at fixed intervals.

Correction: It is misleading to say that Earth's magnetic field reverses every “200,000–300,000 years” on a fixed schedule. Reversals are irregular; some intervals are much shorter while others last for millions of years.

Evidence for past reversals

Molten basalt erupts at mid-ocean ridge → minerals containing magnetic material cool → minerals align with Earth's current field → rocks retain that polarity → seafloor spreads → alternating magnetic stripes form → record of normal and reversed polarity

This is one of the important pieces of evidence supporting seafloor spreading.

Paleomagnetism studies the magnetic record preserved in rocks and sediments to reconstruct past changes in Earth's magnetic field and past positions of continents/plates.

2018 UPSC Prelims: UPSC asked about geomagnetic reversals and correctly treated the statement that Earth's magnetic field has reversed every few hundred thousand years as broadly valid, while also testing the effect of living organisms on the early atmosphere. :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}

Geomagnetic reversal vs geomagnetic excursion

Reversal: long-term polarity changes to the opposite orientation.

Excursion: temporary major deviation of the magnetic field from its normal orientation without becoming a stable long-term reversal.

Memory: R = Reversal = Repeated polarity swap
E = Excursion = temporary “exit” from normal orientation

Magnetosphere and Earth's Protection

The magnetosphere is the region surrounding Earth in which the Earth's magnetic field controls the behaviour of charged particles.

Solar wind reaches Earth → interacts with magnetic field → magnetosphere is compressed on Sun-facing side → extended tail develops away from Sun

Major parts of the magnetosphere

Region Key feature
Bow shock Shock-like boundary where the supersonic solar wind is abruptly slowed and deflected before reaching the magnetopause.
Magnetosheath Turbulent region between the bow shock and magnetopause containing slowed solar-wind plasma.
Magnetopause Boundary where pressure from the solar wind balances pressure associated with Earth's magnetic field.
Magnetotail Long tail extending away from the Sun on the nightside.
Plasmasphere Inner magnetospheric region containing relatively cold, dense plasma linked to Earth's ionosphere.
Polar cusps Funnel-shaped regions near magnetic poles through which some solar-wind energy/particles can enter the magnetosphere.
Van Allen belts Regions of trapped energetic charged particles surrounding Earth.

Bow shock → Magnetosheath → Magnetopause

Sun → solar wind → Bow Shock → Magnetosheath → Magnetopause → Magnetosphere

This sequence is important for understanding how the solar wind interacts with Earth.

Aurora

Aurora is a natural light display produced when energetic charged particles associated with the solar wind and magnetosphere interact with gases in Earth's upper atmosphere.

Solar wind / magnetospheric particles → guided along magnetic field lines → enter upper atmosphere near polar regions → collide with atmospheric atoms and molecules → excitation → light emission → aurora
RegionName
Northern HemisphereAurora Borealis / Northern Lights
Southern HemisphereAurora Australis / Southern Lights

Typical colours depend on the atmospheric gas involved and altitude.

  • Oxygen: commonly produces green and red emissions.
  • Nitrogen: can contribute blue and violet/purple shades.

Auroras are most frequent at high latitudes because charged particles are channelled along magnetic field lines toward polar regions.

Auroral oval

Auroras typically occur in a ring-like region around the magnetic poles called the auroral oval.

During strong geomagnetic storms, auroras can expand towards lower latitudes.

Geomagnetic Storms

A geomagnetic storm is a major disturbance of Earth's magnetosphere caused primarily by enhanced solar activity.

Solar flare / CME → enhanced solar-wind disturbance → interaction with Earth's magnetosphere → magnetospheric compression + currents → geomagnetic storm

Coronal Mass Ejections (CMEs) are particularly important drivers of major geomagnetic storms because they can carry large amounts of magnetised plasma.

Effects

System Possible effect
Atmosphere Enhanced auroral activity and upper-atmospheric changes.
GPS/GNSS Positioning errors and signal disturbances.
HF radio Communication disruption and radio blackouts.
Power grids Geomagnetically induced currents; possible transformer/grid disturbances.
Satellites Radiation hazards, charging effects and increased atmospheric drag in low Earth orbit.
Aviation Communication/navigation disruptions and increased radiation concerns, especially at high latitudes.
Oil and gas pipelines Induced currents may accelerate corrosion and affect monitoring systems.
Mains linkage: Geomagnetic storms demonstrate how an astronomical process can create direct vulnerability in modern technological infrastructure.

Van Allen Radiation Belts

The Van Allen radiation belts are regions around Earth where energetic charged particles are trapped by Earth's magnetic field.

Energetic charged particles → enter/trapped by Earth's magnetic field → spiral along magnetic field lines → mirror between magnetic poles → radiation belts

There are broadly two major belts:

  • Inner belt: dominated mainly by high-energy protons.
  • Outer belt: contains many energetic electrons and is particularly variable during space-weather disturbances.

The belts are important because intense trapped-particle radiation can damage satellites, electronics and spacecraft.

Van Allen belts vs magnetosphere

MagnetosphereVan Allen belts
Entire large region controlled by Earth's magnetic field.Specific regions within the magnetosphere containing trapped energetic particles.
Deflects and guides charged particles.Traps energetic charged particles.
Includes magnetopause, magnetotail, plasmasphere etc.Radiation-belt regions mainly occupied by energetic particles.

Magnetopause, Magnetotail and Polar Cusp: Conceptual Sequence

Sun → solar wind → Bow Shock → Magnetosheath → Magnetopause → inner magnetosphere
Dayside → magnetic field compressed
Nightside → magnetic field stretched → long magnetotail
Magnetic poles → polar cusps → funnel-like entry regions for some solar-wind particles → auroral/magnetospheric processes

Magnetoreception

Magnetoreception is the ability of certain organisms to detect or respond to Earth's magnetic field for orientation and navigation.

Examples include:

  • Migratory birds
  • Sea turtles
  • Salmon
  • Several other animals capable of long-distance navigation
Earth's magnetic field → biological sensing → directional information → migration/navigation
UPSC angle: Magnetoreception links geomagnetism + biology + animal migration.

Magnetic Field of Other Planets

Planet Magnetic-field significance
Mercury Weak global magnetic field despite its small size; linked to its still-active metallic core.
Venus No strong intrinsic global magnetic field comparable to Earth's; has an induced magnetic environment through interaction with the solar wind.
Earth Strong global magnetic field generated by its liquid outer-core geodynamo.
Mars No present-day global dynamo; widespread crustal remanent magnetism records an ancient magnetic field.
Jupiter Extremely strong magnetic field, generated mainly by conducting metallic hydrogen in its deep interior.
Saturn Strong magnetic field generated by conducting fluid in its interior; unusually symmetric magnetic field.
Uranus Strongly tilted and offset magnetic field with an unusual, rapidly changing geometry as the planet rotates.
Neptune Strongly tilted and offset magnetic field, broadly similar in unusual geometry to Uranus.
Prelims comparison: Jupiter's magnetic field is exceptionally strong; Mars preserves evidence of an ancient dynamo; Venus lacks a strong intrinsic global field like Earth.
