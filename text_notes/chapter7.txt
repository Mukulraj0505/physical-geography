## Rocks and Minerals: Basic Framework
-------------------------------------------------------

A rock is a naturally occurring aggregate of one or more minerals held together by physical or chemical bonds. The scientific study of rocks is called petrology.

A mineral is a naturally occurring inorganic solid with an ordered internal/crystalline structure and a characteristic chemical composition or composition range.

Common rock-forming minerals: feldspar, quartz, mica, pyroxene, amphibole and olivine.

[TABLE]
Rock | Mineral idea | Example
Granite | Quartz + feldspar + mica and other minerals | Intrusive igneous
Basalt | Pyroxene + plagioclase + other mafic minerals | Extrusive igneous
Limestone | Primarily calcite | Sedimentary
Marble | Recrystallised calcite/dolomite | Metamorphic
[END TABLE]

Rocks differ in mineral composition, hardness, colour, texture, density and resistance to weathering. These properties influence the landscapes they produce.

## Igneous Rocks
-------------------------------------------------------

Igneous rocks form when molten material magma or lava cools and solidifies. They are often called primary rocks because the first solid rocks of a cooling Earth would have been igneous.

[FLOW]

Magma/lava → cooling → crystallisation + solidification → igneous rock

[/FLOW]

### Intrusive, Extrusive and Hypabyssal Rocks

[TABLE]
Type | Where it forms | Cooling | Texture | Examples
Intrusive / Plutonic | Below Earth's surface | Slow | Coarse-grained; large crystals | Granite, Gabbro, Diorite, Pegmatite
Extrusive / Volcanic | At or very near surface | Rapid | Fine-grained or glassy | Basalt, Andesite, Obsidian, Pumice
Hypabyssal / Dyke-Sill | Shallow subsurface | Intermediate | Intermediate crystal size | Dolerite/Diabase
[END TABLE]

[FLOW]

Deep magma → slow cooling → large crystals → coarse-grained intrusive rock

[/FLOW]

[FLOW]

Surface lava → rapid cooling → tiny crystals → fine-grained extrusive rock

[/FLOW]

Pegmatitic texture: exceptionally large crystals resulting from very slow cooling and crystal growth, often associated with late-stage magmatic fluids.

Glassy texture: extremely rapid cooling prevents normal crystal growth, as in obsidian.

Vesicular texture: gas bubbles become trapped in rapidly cooling lava, producing numerous cavities, as in pumice.

### Acidic/Felsic vs Basic/Mafic Igneous Rocks

[TABLE]
Feature | Acidic / Felsic | Basic / Mafic
Silica | High, generally >~63–65% | Lower, generally ~45–55%
Major elements | Si + Al; relatively low Fe-Mg | Relatively rich in Fe + Mg
Colour | Usually lighter | Usually darker
Density | Generally lower | Generally higher
Viscosity | High | Lower
Lava behaviour | Less fluid; eruptions can be more explosive | More fluid; can travel farther
Examples | Granite, rhyolite | Basalt, gabbro
[END TABLE]

[EXAM FOCUS]

Mnemonic: FEL = Felsic, Elements lighter, Less dense

MAF = Mafic, Magnesium + iron, More dense

[/EXAM FOCUS]

### Important Igneous Textures

[TABLE]
Texture | Meaning | Example
Coarse-grained / Phaneritic | Large visible crystals due to slow cooling at depth. | Granite
Fine-grained / Aphanitic | Small crystals due to rapid cooling. | Basalt
Porphyritic | Large crystals embedded in a finer matrix, indicating more than one cooling stage. | Porphyritic volcanic rocks
Glassy | No well-developed crystals due to extremely rapid cooling. | Obsidian
Vesicular | Numerous gas cavities. | Pumice
Pegmatitic | Exceptionally large crystals. | Pegmatite
[END TABLE]

### Geographical and Economic Significance of Igneous Rocks

* Provide important metallic mineral deposits.

* Influence landscape development because different igneous rocks weather at different rates.

* Provide durable construction material such as granite.

* Volcanic rocks are closely associated with volcanic landscapes and geothermal systems.

* Deccan Traps are extensive flood-basalt formations of peninsular India.

* Weathering of basalt is associated with development of black cotton soil (regur) in large parts of the Deccan region.

[EXAM FOCUS]

UPSC Mains 2022: UPSC directly asked: “Describe the characteristics and types of primary rocks.” Therefore intrusive/extrusive classification, cooling rate, texture, composition and examples are extremely important.

[/EXAM FOCUS]

## Sedimentary Rocks
-------------------------------------------------------

Sedimentary rocks form mainly at or near Earth's surface through accumulation and lithification of sediments, chemical precipitation or accumulation of organic material.

[FLOW]

Weathering → erosion → transportation → deposition → burial → compaction + cementation → lithification → sedimentary rock

[/FLOW]

Sediments may originate from igneous, metamorphic or older sedimentary rocks, as well as from biological remains and chemical precipitation.

### Key characteristics

* Frequently occur in layers or strata.

* May contain fossils, especially where burial is gentle and the sediments are not later subjected to intense metamorphism.

* Often show structures such as bedding, cross-bedding, ripple marks and graded bedding.

* Generally form at relatively low temperatures and pressures compared with metamorphic rocks.

* Important repositories of coal, petroleum, natural gas, groundwater and several mineral deposits.

* They cover a very large proportion of Earth's exposed land surface, although they constitute a much smaller proportion of the total crustal volume.

### Lithification

Lithification is the conversion of loose sediment into solid sedimentary rock through processes such as compaction and cementation.

[FLOW]

Loose sediment → burial → compaction → cementation → lithified sedimentary rock

[/FLOW]

### Types of Sedimentary Rocks

[TABLE]
Type | Formation | Examples
Clastic / Mechanical | Accumulation, compaction and cementation of rock/mineral fragments. | Sandstone, shale, conglomerate, breccia
Chemical | Precipitation of dissolved minerals from water. | Rock salt, gypsum, some limestones, evaporites
Organic / Biogenic | Accumulation or biochemical concentration of remains of organisms. | Coal, chalk, some limestones
[END TABLE]

### Clastic Sedimentary Rocks

[TABLE]
Rock | Characteristic
Conglomerate | Rounded gravel/pebble-sized fragments cemented together.
Breccia | Angular rock fragments cemented together.
Sandstone | Sand-sized particles, commonly rich in quartz and/or feldspar.
Shale | Very fine-grained rock made mainly from compacted clay- and silt-sized particles; commonly fissile.
[END TABLE]

[EXAM FOCUS]

Mnemonic: CBSS → Conglomerate = Bouldery/Rounded, Breccia = Broken/Angular, Sandstone = Sand, Shale = Silt/Clay

[/EXAM FOCUS]

### Chemical and Organic Sedimentary Rocks

* Limestone: mainly calcium carbonate; may be chemical or biochemical/biogenic in origin.

* Dolostone: carbonate rock dominated by the mineral dolomite; commonly develops through alteration of limestone by magnesium-rich fluids.

* Evaporites: form when water evaporates and dissolved salts precipitate. Examples: rock salt and gypsum.

* Coal: forms from accumulated and buried plant material subjected to compaction, heat and pressure over geological time.

* Chalk: soft, porous limestone made largely from microscopic marine calcareous remains.

### Sedimentary Rocks and Resources

[FLOW]

Sedimentary basin → burial + organic accumulation → maturation → hydrocarbons/coal → economic resource

[/FLOW]

* Coal occurs extensively in sedimentary basins.

* Petroleum and natural gas are commonly found in sedimentary basins where source, reservoir and cap rocks occur together under suitable geological conditions.

* Sandstone and limestone can form important aquifers where their pore spaces/fractures permit groundwater storage and movement.

* Sedimentary rocks preserve evidence of past environments, climate, sea-level changes and biological evolution.

## Metamorphic Rocks
-------------------------------------------------------

Metamorphism is the solid-state transformation of a pre-existing rock caused by changes in temperature, pressure, directed stress and/or chemically active fluids.

[CONCEPT CLARIFICATION / CORRECTION]

Critical correction: Metamorphism generally occurs without complete melting. If a rock melts completely, it becomes magma; cooling and solidification of that magma produces an igneous rock.

[/CLARIFICATION]

[FLOW]

Parent rock + heat/pressure/fluids → recrystallisation + mineral/texture changes → metamorphic rock

[/FLOW]

### Factors of Metamorphism

* Heat: promotes recrystallisation and formation of new minerals.

* Pressure: causes compaction and can produce directed mineral alignment.

* Chemically active fluids: facilitate chemical reactions and movement of ions.

* Time: allows mineral reactions and structural changes to proceed over long periods.

* Directed stress: can cause mineral grains to align, producing foliation.

### Foliated vs Non-foliated Metamorphic Rocks

[TABLE]
Foliated | Non-foliated
Minerals show preferred alignment or banding. | No strong planar/banded mineral alignment.
Usually associated with directed pressure/stress. | Often associated with more uniform pressure or mineral-specific recrystallisation.
Slate, Schist, Gneiss | Marble, Quartzite
[END TABLE]

### Parent Rock → Metamorphic Product

[TABLE]
Parent / Protolith | Metamorphic Rock
Shale | Slate → Phyllite → Schist (with increasing metamorphic grade)
Sandstone | Quartzite
Limestone | Marble
Granite | Gneiss
Basalt | Greenschist / Amphibolite / higher-grade products depending on pressure-temperature conditions
Coal | Higher-rank coal; under suitable metamorphic conditions, can progress towards graphite
[END TABLE]

[EXAM FOCUS]

UPSC memory: Shale → Slate | Sandstone → Quartzite | Limestone → Marble | Granite → Gneiss

[/EXAM FOCUS]

### Types of Metamorphism

[TABLE]
Type | Main cause | Typical setting
Contact / Thermal | High temperature from nearby magma intrusion | Around plutons, dykes and sills; forms a contact aureole
Regional | Large-scale heat + pressure + directed stress | Mountain-building and plate-convergence zones
Dynamic / Cataclastic | Mechanical deformation and crushing | Fault zones
[END TABLE]

[FLOW]

Magma intrusion → heat enters surrounding country rock → contact aureole → contact metamorphism

[/FLOW]

[FLOW]

Plate convergence → burial + pressure + heat + deformation → regional metamorphism → mountain belts

[/FLOW]

[FLOW]

Fault movement → crushing + shearing → dynamic metamorphism

[/FLOW]

### Metamorphic Grade and Facies

Metamorphic grade indicates the intensity of metamorphic conditions experienced by a rock.

[FLOW]

Low grade → increasing temperature/pressure → medium grade → high grade

[/FLOW]

Metamorphic facies represent characteristic mineral assemblages formed under particular ranges of pressure and temperature.

Examples include greenschist, amphibolite, granulite, blueschist and eclogite facies.

[EXAM FOCUS]

Mains use: Metamorphic facies help reconstruct the pressure–temperature history of rocks and therefore provide evidence about burial, subduction, crustal thickening and tectonic evolution.

[/EXAM FOCUS]

## Rock Cycle
-------------------------------------------------------

The rock cycle describes the continuous transformation and recycling of rocks through geological processes. It is not a rigid one-way sequence.

[FLOW]

Magma → cooling + solidification → Igneous Rock

[/FLOW]

[FLOW]

Igneous / Sedimentary / Metamorphic Rock → weathering + erosion → sediments → deposition + lithification → Sedimentary Rock

[/FLOW]

[FLOW]

Igneous / Sedimentary / Metamorphic Rock → heat + pressure + fluids → Metamorphic Rock

[/FLOW]

[FLOW]

Any rock → melting → magma → cooling → Igneous Rock

[/FLOW]

[FLOW]

Any exposed rock → weathering → sediments → Sedimentary Rock

[/FLOW]

Thus:

[FLOW]

Igneous ↔ Sedimentary ↔ Metamorphic ↔ Magma ↔ Igneous

[/FLOW]

[CONCEPT CLARIFICATION / CORRECTION]

Important: A sedimentary rock does not have to become metamorphic before becoming igneous. It can be buried, melt directly and then cool into an igneous rock.

[/CLARIFICATION]

[EXAM FOCUS]

UPSC Prelims 2016: UPSC tested the rock cycle as the continuous transformation of one rock type into another under varying geological conditions.

[/EXAM FOCUS]

### Rock Cycle and Plate Tectonics

[FLOW]

Weathering + erosion → sediment → deposition

[/FLOW]

[FLOW]

Burial + heat + pressure → metamorphism

[/FLOW]

[FLOW]

Subduction → deeper burial/heating → melting → magma

[/FLOW]

[FLOW]

Magma → volcanism/intrusion → igneous rock

[/FLOW]

This creates an important link between rock cycle + plate tectonics + mountain building + volcanism + erosion.

## Rock Properties and Geographical Significance
-------------------------------------------------------

### Weathering and Landforms

Rock resistance controls the relative rate at which landscapes are eroded.

[FLOW]

Different rock hardness/composition → differential weathering → differential erosion → variation in relief → hills, ridges, valleys and escarpments

[/FLOW]

Granite: resistant rock; can form uplands and exposed tors under suitable conditions.

Basalt: extensive lava plateaus such as the Deccan Plateau/Deccan Traps.

Limestone: soluble in weakly acidic water; can develop karst landscapes, caves, sinkholes and underground drainage.

Shale: relatively weak and fine-grained; commonly erodes more easily than resistant sandstone or limestone.

### Rocks, soil and groundwater

[FLOW]

Parent rock → weathering → mineral material → soil formation

[/FLOW]

[FLOW]

Permeable rock → infiltration/storage → groundwater/aquifer

[/FLOW]

[FLOW]

Impermeable layer → restricts downward movement → groundwater may accumulate above it

[/FLOW]

Rock type therefore influences soil fertility, drainage, groundwater availability, vegetation and agriculture.

## Common Prelims Traps
-------------------------------------------------------

[TABLE]
Trap | Correct concept
Basalt is intrusive. | Basalt = extrusive volcanic rock.
Gabbro is extrusive. | Gabbro = intrusive, coarse-grained equivalent of basalt.
Granite forms by rapid cooling. | Granite forms by slow cooling at depth.
Metamorphism involves complete melting. | Usually solid-state transformation without complete melting.
Marble is sedimentary. | Marble = metamorphosed limestone.
Quartzite is metamorphosed limestone. | Quartzite = metamorphosed sandstone.
Gneiss is sedimentary. | Gneiss = metamorphic rock.
Foliation means sedimentary layering. | Foliation = aligned metamorphic minerals due to directed stress.
Conglomerate and breccia are the same. | Conglomerate = rounded clasts; Breccia = angular clasts.
Rock cycle follows one fixed route. | Non-linear and continuous.
Sedimentary rock must become metamorphic before becoming igneous. | No. It can melt directly → magma → igneous rock.
All sedimentary rocks contain fossils. | Fossils are common in sedimentary rocks but not every sedimentary rock is fossiliferous.
All limestone is purely chemical. | Limestone can have chemical, biochemical and other origins.
Acidic rock means chemically acidic like an acid solution. | “Acidic” in igneous petrology refers mainly to high silica content.
Acidic lava travels farther because it is highly fluid. | High-silica acidic/felsic lava is viscous and generally less mobile.
Weathering resistance is the same for all rocks. | Rock composition, texture, fractures and climate affect differential weathering.
[END TABLE]
