# Chapter 15 — Temperature: Distribution & Variations
=======================================================

-------------------------------------------------------
## Temperature: Meaning & Basic Concepts
-------------------------------------------------------

Temperature is a measure of the average kinetic energy of particles in a substance. It indicates the degree of hotness or coldness of a body or environment.

* Higher temperature → particles have greater average kinetic energy.

* Lower temperature → particles have lower average kinetic energy.

* Surface temperature refers to the temperature of the physical surface such as land, soil or water.

* Air temperature refers to the temperature of the surrounding atmosphere, generally measured at a standard height above the surface.

[TABLE]
Feature | Surface Temperature | Air Temperature
Meaning | Temperature of the surface itself. | Temperature of the air.
Measurement | Can vary sharply according to surface material. | Measured using instruments at a standard exposure height.
Variation | Usually more rapid and extreme. | Generally more gradual.
Main controls | Radiation, surface characteristics, moisture. | Radiation, convection, advection, atmospheric moisture and circulation.
[END TABLE]

### Temperature Scales

[TABLE]
Conversion | Formula
Celsius → Fahrenheit | F = (9/5)C + 32
Fahrenheit → Celsius | C = (F − 32) × 5/9
Celsius → Kelvin | K = C + 273.15
Kelvin → Celsius | C = K − 273.15
[END TABLE]

[EXAM FOCUS]

UPSC fact: Temperature is normally measured in °C, while the SI unit of thermodynamic temperature is Kelvin (K).

[/EXAM FOCUS]

## Temperature Measurement
-------------------------------------------------------

[TABLE]
Instrument | Main Use / Principle
Thermometer | Measurement of temperature.
Thermocouple | Generates electrical voltage because of a temperature difference between dissimilar metals.
Resistance Temperature Detector (RTD) | Uses change in electrical resistance with temperature.
Thermistor | Uses temperature-dependent electrical resistance.
Pyrometer | Measures high temperatures through emitted thermal radiation.
Gas thermometer | Uses expansion/pressure characteristics of a gas.
[END TABLE]

## Temperature Variation
-------------------------------------------------------

Temperature varies over different time scales and across different spatial scales.

[FLOW]

Diurnal → Seasonal → Annual → Regional → Global

[/FLOW]

### Diurnal Variation

Diurnal variation refers to changes in temperature over a 24-hour period.

* Daytime: solar heating of the surface increases.

* Night: incoming solar radiation stops and the surface loses energy through longwave radiation.

* The minimum temperature usually occurs shortly after sunrise, not exactly at midnight.

* The maximum temperature usually occurs in the afternoon, often around 2–4 PM depending on local conditions.

### Seasonal Variation

Seasonal temperature variation occurs because Earth's axis is tilted and Earth revolves around the Sun.

[FLOW]

Axial tilt + Revolution
↓
Changing solar angle + changing day length
↓
Seasonal temperature variation

[/FLOW]

## Isopleths
-------------------------------------------------------

Isopleths are lines joining places having the same value of a particular variable.

[TABLE]
Term | Meaning
Isotherm | Equal temperature.
Isobar | Equal atmospheric pressure.
Isohyet | Equal rainfall/precipitation.
Isohaline | Equal salinity.
Isobath | Equal depth below sea level.
Isohyps | Equal elevation/height.
Isopycnic | Equal density.
Isochron | Equal travel time / equal time interval.
Isopach | Equal thickness of a geological formation.
Isogonal | Equal magnetic declination.
[END TABLE]

## 1. Latitude
-------------------------------------------------------

Latitude is one of the most important controls of temperature because it influences the angle of incidence of solar radiation and the duration of sunlight.

* Near the Equator, solar rays are more nearly vertical.

* Toward the poles, solar rays become increasingly oblique.

* Oblique rays spread the same energy over a larger area.

* They also pass through a greater thickness of atmosphere.

* Therefore, average temperature generally decreases from low to high latitudes.

[FLOW]

Equator → more direct rays → concentrated energy → warmer
Poles → oblique rays → dispersed energy → colder

[/FLOW]

[EXAM FOCUS]

But remember: Latitude alone does not explain actual temperature at a location. Ocean currents, elevation, winds, continentality and other factors modify the basic latitudinal pattern.

[/EXAM FOCUS]

-------------------------------------------------------
## 2. Altitude
-------------------------------------------------------

Temperature generally decreases with increasing altitude in the troposphere.

The average environmental lapse rate is approximately:

[FLOW]

6.5°C per 1000 m (6.5°C/km)

[/FLOW]

* Atmospheric pressure decreases with height.

* Air becomes thinner.

* The lower atmosphere receives much of its heat indirectly from Earth's surface.

### Why Does Temperature Decrease with Height?

[FLOW]

Earth surface absorbs shortwave
↓
Surface emits longwave radiation
↓
Lower atmosphere receives much of its heat from below
↓
Heating generally decreases upward
↓
Temperature falls with altitude in the troposphere

[/FLOW]

[EXAM FOCUS]

Important distinction: The environmental lapse rate is the actual rate at which atmospheric temperature decreases with height at a particular time and place. It is not always exactly 6.5°C/km.

[/EXAM FOCUS]

### Air Temperature vs Elevation

Because elevation affects temperature, two places at the same latitude may have very different temperatures.

Example: A high-elevation city near the Equator can be much cooler than a lowland tropical city at nearly the same latitude.

-------------------------------------------------------
## 3. Albedo, Cloud Cover and Ice
-------------------------------------------------------

Albedo is the fraction of incoming solar radiation reflected by a surface.

[FLOW]

High albedo → more reflection → less absorption → cooling tendency

[/FLOW]

[FLOW]

Low albedo → less reflection → more absorption → warming tendency

[/FLOW]

[TABLE]
Surface | General albedo tendency
Fresh snow | Very high
Ice | High
Clouds | Generally high reflection of incoming solar radiation
Vegetation | Moderate/relatively low
Dark ocean surface | Low, especially at high Sun angles
[END TABLE]

### Clouds Have a Dual Effect

[TABLE]
Day | Night
Reflect incoming solar radiation → cooling tendency. | Absorb/re-emit terrestrial longwave radiation → reduces nighttime cooling.
[END TABLE]

[EXAM FOCUS]

UPSC trap: Clouds do not necessarily mean cooling under all conditions. Their effect depends on the balance between reflected shortwave radiation and retained longwave radiation.

[/EXAM FOCUS]

### Ice–Albedo Feedback

[FLOW]

Warming
↓
Ice/Snow melt
↓
Albedo decreases
↓
More solar energy absorbed
↓
Further warming
↓
More ice/snow melt

[/FLOW]

This is a classic example of a positive climate feedback.

-------------------------------------------------------
## 4. Differential Heating of Land and Water
-------------------------------------------------------

Land and water heat and cool at different rates. This produces the land–sea thermal contrast.

[TABLE]
Land | Water
Heats faster. | Heats more slowly.
Cools faster. | Cools more slowly.
Generally greater temperature range. | Generally smaller temperature range.
Strong continental effect. | Strong maritime/moderating effect.
[END TABLE]

### Why Does Water Heat Slowly?

* Water has a high heat capacity.

* Solar energy can penetrate into a layer of water rather than being concentrated only at the surface.

* Waves and convection distribute heat vertically.

* Evaporation removes substantial heat.

[FLOW]

Land = Faster heating/cooling
Water = Slower heating/cooling

[/FLOW]

Mnemonic: LAND = FAST; WATER = SLOW.

-------------------------------------------------------
## Continentality vs Maritime Influence
-------------------------------------------------------

Continentality refers to the influence of large landmasses, while maritime influence refers to the moderating effect of nearby water bodies.

[FLOW]

Interior continental areas → hotter summers + colder winters → large annual range
Coastal areas → cooler summers + milder winters → smaller annual range

[/FLOW]

### Annual Temperature Range

Annual temperature range = Mean temperature of warmest month − Mean temperature of coldest month.

* Generally larger in continental interiors.

* Generally smaller near oceans.

* The difference is especially pronounced in the Northern Hemisphere because it contains much more land at middle and high latitudes.

-------------------------------------------------------
## 5. Prevailing Winds
-------------------------------------------------------

Winds transport heat horizontally through advection.

* Winds from warmer regions can raise temperature.

* Winds from colder regions can lower temperature.

* Marine winds can moderate temperatures near coasts.

[FLOW]

Warm-source air → warmer destination
Cold-source air → cooler destination

[/FLOW]

### Example

Western Europe is warmer than many places at similar latitudes because of the combined influence of the North Atlantic Drift and prevailing westerlies.

-------------------------------------------------------
## 6. Ocean Currents
-------------------------------------------------------

Ocean currents transport enormous quantities of heat and therefore influence the temperature of adjacent coastal regions.

[TABLE]
Current | Nature | General Temperature Effect
Gulf Stream / North Atlantic Drift | Warm | Warms adjacent western Europe.
Kuroshio | Warm | Raises temperatures along parts of Japan.
California Current | Cold | Cooling influence on western North America.
Canary Current | Cold | Cooling influence along northwest Africa.
Peru/Humboldt Current | Cold | Cooling influence along western South America.
[END TABLE]

[FLOW]

Warm current → warmer adjacent coast
Cold current → cooler adjacent coast

[/FLOW]

### Ocean Current + Wind = Coastal Climate

The effect of an ocean current is often amplified or modified by the prevailing wind direction.

Example: A warm ocean current transporting heat toward a coast, combined with onshore winds, can strongly moderate the coastal climate.

-------------------------------------------------------
## 7. Aspect of Slope
-------------------------------------------------------

Aspect means the direction towards which a slope faces.

### Northern Hemisphere

* South-facing slopes: generally receive more direct sunlight.

* North-facing slopes: generally receive less direct sunlight.

[EXAM FOCUS]

Reverse in Southern Hemisphere: North-facing slopes generally receive more direct solar radiation than south-facing slopes.

[/EXAM FOCUS]

### Factors Controlling the Effect of Aspect

* Angle of incidence of sunlight.

* Duration of sunshine.

* Season.

* Latitude.

* Slope steepness.

* Cloud cover.

### Environmental Importance

Aspect affects:

[FLOW]

Aspect
↓
Insolation
↓
Temperature + Evaporation + Soil Moisture
↓
Vegetation + Snow Persistence + Agriculture

[/FLOW]

-------------------------------------------------------
## 8. Day–Night Cycle
-------------------------------------------------------

Earth's rotation produces the daily cycle of heating and cooling.

[FLOW]

Sunrise → heating begins
↓
Afternoon → maximum surface temperature generally occurs
↓
Sunset → cooling begins
↓
Pre-dawn → minimum temperature generally occurs

[/FLOW]

### Why Does Maximum Temperature Occur After Noon?

Earth's surface continues receiving more energy than it loses for some time after solar noon. Therefore, maximum temperature generally occurs in the afternoon rather than exactly at noon.

[EXAM FOCUS]

UPSC concept: Solar noon ≠ maximum daily temperature.

[/EXAM FOCUS]

-------------------------------------------------------
## Daily Temperature Cycle
-------------------------------------------------------

[TABLE]
Time | Radiation Balance | Temperature
Morning | Incoming solar radiation increasing. | Rising.
Solar noon | Sun highest, but surface energy storage continues. | Still rising.
Afternoon | Surface heat storage reaches maximum. | Maximum temperature usually occurs.
Night | No incoming solar radiation; outgoing radiation continues. | Falling.
Near sunrise | Maximum nocturnal cooling has occurred. | Minimum temperature generally occurs shortly after sunrise.
[END TABLE]

-------------------------------------------------------
## Temperature Anomalies
-------------------------------------------------------

Temperature anomaly means the difference between an observed temperature and the long-term normal temperature for that place and period.

[FLOW]

Temperature anomaly = Observed temperature − Normal temperature

[/FLOW]

[TABLE]
Type | Meaning
Positive anomaly | Observed temperature is above normal.
Negative anomaly | Observed temperature is below normal.
[END TABLE]

Example: Normal temperature = 25°C; observed temperature = 27°C.

[FLOW]

Anomaly = 27 − 25 = +2°C

[/FLOW]

## Urban Heat Island (UHI)
-------------------------------------------------------

An Urban Heat Island is a built-up urban area that is warmer than its less-developed or rural surroundings.

The phenomenon was studied extensively by Luke Howard in London, while the term “urban heat island” is associated with later urban-climatology literature.

### Why Are Cities Hotter?

* Concrete and asphalt: store large amounts of heat.

* Low vegetation: reduces evapotranspiration and natural cooling.

* Anthropogenic heat: vehicles, buildings, industries and air-conditioning systems release heat.

* Urban canyon effect: buildings alter radiation exchange and ventilation.

* Impervious surfaces: reduce evaporation of water.

* Reduced wind: dense construction restricts heat dispersion.

* Air pollution: alters radiation and atmospheric conditions.

[FLOW]

Vegetation ↓ + Dark surfaces ↑ + Heat storage ↑ + Anthropogenic heat ↑
↓
Urban Heat Island

[/FLOW]

### Types of Urban Heat Islands

[TABLE]
Type | Meaning
Surface UHI | Difference in land-surface temperature between urban and rural areas.
Canopy Layer UHI | Urban warming within the layer extending roughly from the surface to building/vegetation canopy level.
Boundary Layer UHI | Urban warming extending upward into the lower atmosphere above the urban canopy.
[END TABLE]

### UHI — Major Impacts

* Higher nighttime temperatures.

* Greater cooling and electricity demand.

* Increased heat stress.

* Changes in local precipitation and convection.

* Interaction with air pollution and ozone formation.

* Greater vulnerability during heat waves.

### UHI Mitigation

[FLOW]

Green roofs + Urban trees + Cool roofs + Reflective surfaces
+ Water-sensitive design + Better ventilation
↓
Lower urban heat storage
↓
Reduced UHI

[/FLOW]

### Key Indian Applications

* Urban greening.

* Cool/reflective roofs.

* Permeable surfaces.

* Water bodies and blue-green infrastructure.

* Heat Action Plans.

* Reduced anthropogenic heat emissions.

## Horizontal Distribution of Temperature
-------------------------------------------------------

The horizontal distribution of temperature refers to the variation of temperature across Earth's surface from one location to another.

This is commonly represented through isotherms.

### General Rule

[FLOW]

Temperature generally decreases from Equator → Poles

[/FLOW]

However, actual isotherms rarely follow latitude perfectly because of:

* Continents and oceans.

* Ocean currents.

* Prevailing winds.

* Altitude.

* Cloud cover.

* Relief.

-------------------------------------------------------
## Isotherms
-------------------------------------------------------

Isotherms are lines joining places having the same temperature.

### General Characteristics

* They tend to run approximately east–west because of the basic latitudinal temperature gradient.

* They deviate substantially where land and water contrasts are strong.

* They bend around warm and cold ocean currents.

* Spacing of isotherms indicates the temperature gradient.

[FLOW]

Close isotherms → Steep temperature gradient
Wide isotherm spacing → Gentle temperature gradient

[/FLOW]

-------------------------------------------------------
## Why Do Isotherms Bend?
-------------------------------------------------------

### 1. Land–Water Contrast

Land heats and cools faster than water, producing different temperatures at the same latitude.

### 2. Ocean Currents

* Warm currents push isotherms towards higher latitudes.

* Cold currents push isotherms towards lower latitudes.

### 3. Prevailing Winds

Winds transport warm or cold air masses and therefore modify the latitudinal temperature pattern.

-------------------------------------------------------
## January Isotherms
-------------------------------------------------------

January represents winter in the Northern Hemisphere and summer in the Southern Hemisphere.

### Northern Hemisphere — January

* Large continents are strongly cooled.

* Oceans remain relatively warmer.

* Isotherms generally bend equatorward over continents.

* They generally bend poleward over oceans.

[FLOW]

January NH:
Cold continents → equatorward bend
Warm oceans → poleward bend

[/FLOW]

### Important Regions

* Northern Siberia experiences extremely low winter temperatures.

* Western Europe remains relatively mild because of maritime influence and warm North Atlantic waters.

* Large annual temperature ranges occur in continental interiors.

-------------------------------------------------------
## July Isotherms
-------------------------------------------------------

July represents summer in the Northern Hemisphere and winter in the Southern Hemisphere.

### Northern Hemisphere — July

* Continental interiors become strongly heated.

* Isotherms generally bend poleward over continents.

* They generally bend equatorward over relatively cooler oceans.

[FLOW]

July NH:
Hot continents → poleward bend
Cooler oceans → equatorward bend

[/FLOW]

-------------------------------------------------------
## January vs July Isotherms
-------------------------------------------------------

[TABLE]
Feature | January | July
NH land | Strong cooling | Strong heating
NH continental isotherms | Bend equatorward | Bend poleward
NH ocean influence | Relatively warmer | Relatively cooler
Main seasonal control | Winter in NH | Summer in NH
[END TABLE]

Mnemonic: COLD–HOT

[FLOW]

JAN = Land cold → isotherms go toward Equator
JUL = Land hot → isotherms go toward Pole

[/FLOW]

## Why Is the Southern Hemisphere More Uniform Thermally?
-------------------------------------------------------

The Southern Hemisphere has a much larger proportion of ocean surface, particularly in the middle and high latitudes.

* Oceans heat and cool slowly.

* Ocean currents redistribute heat.

* Therefore, annual temperature contrasts are generally smaller than in the Northern Hemisphere.

[EXAM FOCUS]

UPSC favourite: The Northern Hemisphere generally has a greater annual temperature range than the Southern Hemisphere because of its much larger landmass.

[/EXAM FOCUS]

## Latitudinal Temperature Zones
-------------------------------------------------------

[TABLE]
Broad Zone | General Character
Equatorial | High temperatures, small annual range.
Tropical/Subtropical | High temperatures; subtropical deserts can experience intense summer heat.
Temperate | Moderate temperatures with stronger seasonal variation.
Polar | Low temperatures and strong seasonal effects at high latitudes.
[END TABLE]

-------------------------------------------------------
## Temperature Range
-------------------------------------------------------

Temperature range is the numerical difference between the maximum and minimum temperature values over a given period.

[TABLE]
Type | Meaning
Diurnal range | Maximum temperature − Minimum temperature within one day.
Monthly range | Difference between temperature values across a month.
Annual range | Mean temperature of hottest month − Mean temperature of coldest month.
[END TABLE]

### Where Is Temperature Range Large?

* Continental interiors.

* Dry regions with clear skies.

* Areas far from oceanic moderation.

### Where Is Temperature Range Small?

* Coastal regions.

* Island environments.

* Humid/cloudy regions.

* Areas strongly influenced by oceans.

-------------------------------------------------------
## Temperature and Deserts
-------------------------------------------------------

Many subtropical desert interiors experience extreme daytime heating because of:

* Clear skies.

* Low humidity.

* Strong incoming solar radiation.

* Limited cloud cover.

At night, however, clear skies also permit rapid longwave heat loss, resulting in substantial diurnal temperature ranges.

[FLOW]

Clear + dry desert
→ Strong daytime heating
→ Strong nighttime radiative cooling
→ Large diurnal range

[/FLOW]

-------------------------------------------------------
## Temperature Inversion — PYQ Enrichment
-------------------------------------------------------

A temperature inversion occurs when temperature increases with height over a particular layer instead of normally decreasing with height.

### Common Conditions for Surface/Radiation Inversion

[FLOW]

Clear sky + Calm winds + Long night
↓
Rapid radiative cooling of ground
↓
Air near surface cools strongly
↓
Warmer air remains above
↓
Temperature inversion

[/FLOW]

### Effects

* Suppresses vertical mixing.

* Traps pollutants near the surface.

* May intensify fog and smog.

* Can adversely affect air quality in cities and valleys.

[EXAM FOCUS]

Classic UPSC statement: Clear skies and calm conditions favour radiation inversion because they permit strong surface cooling and suppress vertical mixing.

[/EXAM FOCUS]

-------------------------------------------------------
## Mean Annual Temperature Distribution
-------------------------------------------------------

The mean annual temperature is the average of the monthly mean temperatures of a year.

### Broad Pattern

* Highest annual mean temperatures occur in the tropical/subtropical belt, but the thermal equator may shift seasonally and the hottest areas are not necessarily located directly on the Equator.

* Temperatures generally decrease towards the poles.

* Continental interiors can show extreme temperature contrasts.

* Oceanic regions are more thermally moderate.

-------------------------------------------------------
## Thermal Equator
-------------------------------------------------------

The thermal equator is the line connecting locations with the highest mean temperatures at a particular time or period.

* It does not necessarily coincide with the geographic Equator.

* It shifts northward during Northern Hemisphere summer.

* It shifts southward during Southern Hemisphere summer.

* Its position is influenced by the distribution of land and water and seasonal heating.

## Climate Change and Temperature Distribution
-------------------------------------------------------

Climate change is altering Earth's temperature profile across different regions rather than producing identical warming everywhere.

### Major Observed/Expected Patterns

* Increase in global mean temperature.

* Changes in frequency and intensity of heat extremes.

* Strong warming in many high-latitude regions.

* Changes in seasonal temperature patterns.

* Changes in land–ocean thermal contrast.

* Impacts on snow, ice and permafrost.

### Polar Amplification

Polar regions can warm faster than the global average because of interacting feedbacks such as ice–albedo feedback, changes in snow cover and other cryosphere–atmosphere interactions.

[FLOW]

Warming
→ Ice/Snow loss
→ Albedo ↓
→ Absorption ↑
→ Further warming

[/FLOW]

-------------------------------------------------------
## Temperature and Different Climatic Regions
-------------------------------------------------------

### Tropical Regions

* High solar input.

* Small annual temperature range in many humid equatorial regions.

* Strong convection and cloud formation can modify surface temperatures.

### Temperate Regions

* Stronger seasonal variation.

* Greater distinction between summer and winter.

* Strong influence of westerlies and ocean currents in many regions.

### Polar Regions

* Low solar angle.

* Long periods of daylight/darkness depending on season.

* High albedo from snow and ice.

* Very low temperatures, especially in continental interiors.

## Most Important UPSC Traps
-------------------------------------------------------

[TABLE]
Wrong Intuition | Correct Concept
Maximum temperature occurs at noon. | Usually occurs in the afternoon because surface heat storage continues after solar noon.
Maximum temperature occurs exactly at the Equator. | Not necessarily; clouds, humidity, convection, deserts and land–sea effects modify temperature.
Higher latitude always means proportionally colder conditions. | Latitude is a major control, but currents, altitude, winds and continentality modify the pattern.
Cloudy nights are colder. | Cloud cover usually reduces nighttime radiative cooling.
High albedo means stronger heating. | High albedo means greater reflection.
Land and water heat equally. | Land generally heats/cools faster; water has stronger thermal inertia.
Warm ocean currents cool nearby coasts. | Warm currents generally warm adjacent coastal areas.
Cold currents always produce rain. | Cold currents often stabilise the lower atmosphere and can suppress rainfall; coastal fog may occur instead.
UHI means global warming. | UHI is a local/regional urban phenomenon; it can amplify the effects of background warming.
Temperature always decreases with altitude. | Normally in the troposphere, but temperature inversion reverses the vertical gradient locally.
[END TABLE]
